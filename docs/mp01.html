A Bigger Library, But Not a Bigger Audience: Rethinking Netflix’s Content Strategy for 2027

Looking through Netflix’s viewing data from January 2023 to June 2026, which covers 24,427 titles, one thing stands out to me. For the past few years we have been adding more and more to the catalog, and audiences have not responded with the same enthusiasm.

Start with the size of the library. Between the second half of 2023 and the first half of 2026, the number of titles grew from 6,595 to 8,187, an increase of about 24%. Over the same stretch, total hours viewed rose by only 14.5%. We added nearly a quarter more content, yet the time people spend watching did not keep pace, which means each title is now getting less attention on average. Growing the catalog for its own sake is starting to pay off less and less.

So where is the time actually going? The obvious guess would be a handful of blockbusters, but the numbers say otherwise. In the first half of 2026, the top ten titles together accounted for just 6.70% of all viewing hours. What really holds the platform up is a group of strong performers that may never hit number one but keep drawing viewers period after period.

Franchises carry the most weight in that group. The Bridgerton universe has racked up 4.86 billion hours in total, and the core seasons of Stranger Things have reached 5.59 billion. Stranger Things 4 alone has appeared in seven consecutive reporting periods, and even in its weakest period it still pulled in 97.1 million hours, the steadiest record of any long-running title. A show that people keep coming back to for more than three years is worth far more than its opening weeks suggest.

The second pattern worth paying attention to is reach. Titles available worldwide make up only 35.1% of the catalog, but they account for 51.9% of viewing hours. Put simply, a globally available title earns roughly twice as many hours as a regionally licensed one.

What I find most interesting is that many of the most consistent performers are not in English. The Korean drama Queen of Tears stayed on the charts for five periods, averaging 221 million hours per period, with its lowest period still at 61.1 million. When Life Gives You Tangerines averaged 249 million hours. These are stories rooted firmly in Korea, and yet audiences everywhere embraced them. Local content does not have to stay local. The better approach is to produce locally and distribute globally.

With that in mind, I have two recommendations for original content in 2027 and beyond.

First, new projects should launch with global rights wherever possible. Regional licensing still has a role to play, but it should no longer be the main path. Markets like Korea, which have already shown they can turn local stories into worldwide hits, deserve more investment.

Second, roughly half of the content budget should go toward flagship franchises that can run for multiple seasons and support spin-offs. On the other side, mid-budget, one-off projects with no realistic sequel potential should be cut. Rather than releasing hundreds more titles each year that are quickly forgotten, we would be better off putting that money toward the next Stranger Things or Bridgerton.

As for live events, they can gather a large audience in a short window, which makes them useful for generating buzz and bringing in new subscribers. But they contribute relatively few viewing hours. Live programming works best as a supporting tool that points viewers toward our long-running shows, not as a replacement for them.

In short, what we need over the next few years is not more content. We need content that can travel across borders and still hold an audience years later.

read_all_sheets <- function(p) {
  excel_sheets(p) |>
    set_names() |>
    map(\(s) read_excel(p, sheet = s, skip = 3, na = "*") |>
              mutate(`Release Date` = as.Date(`Release Date`),
                     Runtime        = as.character(Runtime))) |>
    list_rbind(names_to = "sheet")
}
load_netflix_data <- function(){
  library(tidyverse)
  library(rvest)
  library(readxl)
  library(fs)
  
  data_dir <- path("data", "mp01")
    
  if(!dir_exists(data_dir)){
    dir_create(data_dir, showWarnings=FALSE, recurse=TRUE)
  }
  
  NETFLIX_URLS <- list(
    `2023H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-a-netflix-engagement-report",
    `2023H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2023",
    `2024H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2024",
    `2024H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2024",
    `2025H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2025",
    `2025H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2025",
    `2026H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2026"
  )
  
  download_if_needed <- function(url, local){
    if(!file_exists(local)){
      download.file(url, local, quiet = TRUE, mode = "wb")
    }
    
    return(local)
  }
    
  NETFLIX_URLS |> 
    map(read_html) |> 
    map(html_element, xpath = "//a[contains(@href, '.xlsx')]") |> 
    map_chr(html_attr, "href") |> 
    enframe("period", "url") |> 
   mutate(path = path(data_dir, paste0(period, ".xlsx")), 
          path = map2(url, path, download_if_needed), 
          data = map(path, read_excel, skip=3, na="*"), 
          data = map(data, \(x) x |> mutate(`Release Date` = as.Date(`Release Date`)))) |>
    unnest(data) |> 
    select(-url, -path) |> 
    mutate(year=str_sub(period, 1, 4), 
           half=str_sub(period, start=6), 
           period_start = make_date(year, if_else(half==1, 1, 7), 1), 
           period_end = make_date(year, if_else(half==1, 6, 12), if_else(half==1, 30, 31))) |> 
    filter(!is.na(`Hours Viewed`)) |> 
    mutate(live_event = !is.na(Runtime) & str_detect(Runtime, "\\d+:\\d+\\*"), 
           clean_time = str_remove(Runtime, fixed("*")),
           Runtime = 60 * hour(hm(clean_time)) + minute(hm(clean_time))) |>
    select(-clean_time) |>
    mutate(Runtime = coalesce(Runtime, mean(Runtime, na.rm=TRUE)), 
           .by=Title, 
           Title = stringi::stri_trans_general(Title, "Latin-ASCII"),
           Title = str_remove(Title, " // .*"), 
           Title = str_remove(Title, "[^[:ascii:]].*"), 
           Title = str_trim(Title)) |>
    filter_out(Title == "") |>
    select(year, 
           period, 
           period_start, 
           period_end, 
           Title, 
           -half, 
           Runtime, 
           Views, 
           `Hours Viewed`, 
           `Release Date`,
           `Available Globally?`, 
           live_event)
}

show_vs_movie_trends <- NETFLIX_DATA_CLEAN |>

    id_cols = period,
